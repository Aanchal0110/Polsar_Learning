Fundamentals

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)
SAR uses a small antenna to transmit radar pulses, creating a synthetic aperture by combining multiple echoes. SAR systems simulate a large antenna by integrating echoes from multiple positions. It enables high-resolution imaging in all-weather conditions, ideal for remote sensing. Read more
Range, Azimuth, and Swath
Range is perpendicular to the flight path, azimuth is parallel, and swath is the imaged area on Earth's surface. The range direction measures distance based on time delay, while azimuth depends on Doppler frequency shifts. Swath width varies with wavelength and platform altitude. Read more
Backscattering
Reflection of radar signals toward the sensor; it depends on surface roughness, wavelength, and polarization. Backscatter is influenced by dielectric properties and surface geometry. Stronger backscatter often indicates rough or wet surfaces. Read more
Types of Backscattering
Includes single bounce (smooth surfaces), double bounce (man-made structures), and volume bounce (vegetation). These scattering types help differentiate surface types. For example, double bounce is strong between buildings and the ground. Read more
Polarimetric SAR (PolSAR)
PolSAR transmits and receives in multiple polarizations (HH, VV, HV, VH) for detailed target analysis. It allows discrimination of land features and scattering mechanisms, making PolSAR powerful for vegetation, urban, and soil studies. Read more
Types of Polarization
Includes single, dual, and quad polarization for capturing different scattering properties. Single uses one polarization; dual uses two (e.g., HH and HV); quad uses all four combinations (HH, VV, HV, VH) for advanced analysis. Read more
Polarization Correlation
Examines relationships between polarization states: co-pol (HH, VV) and cross-pol (HV, VH). High correlation between HH and VV often indicates smooth or homogeneous surfaces, while low correlation can suggest vegetation or rough terrain. Read more
Scattering Matrix
A 2×2 matrix describing how radar signals change polarization after interacting with a target. It contains four elements: SHH, SHV, SVH, SVV, representing transmission and reception in horizontal/vertical polarizations. Read more
Covariance Matrix
Used in PolSAR systems to analyze statistical relationships between polarization channels. It helps in understanding the power and correlation between different polarimetric components. It's essential for classification and decomposition techniques. Read more
Decomposition
Breaks down radar signals into surface, double-bounce, and volume scattering components. These components help identify and classify land cover types and surface features in PolSAR imagery using models like Freeman-Durden or Cloude-Pottier. Read more
Interferometry (InSAR)
Uses phase differences between radar images to measure surface deformation and topography. By comparing two SAR images taken at different times, InSAR can detect ground movement, such as landslides or subsidence, and produce high-resolution elevation maps. Read more
Digital Elevation Model (DEM)
A 3D representation of Earth's surface created using SAR or InSAR data. DEMs are widely used in topographic analysis, flood simulation, urban planning, and geoscience. InSAR-derived DEMs provide highly accurate terrain data. Read more
Speckle Filtering
Reduces noise in SAR images while preserving important features and edges. Speckle is a granular noise inherent to coherent imaging systems. Filters like Lee, Frost, and Gamma MAP are used to improve image clarity and analysis quality. Read more
Co-registration
Aligns multiple SAR images for accurate change detection and interferometric analysis. It ensures that corresponding pixels from different dates or sensors represent the same ground point. This step is crucial for time series analysis and InSAR. Read more
Geo-coding
Transforms radar image coordinates into geographic coordinates using projection and correction. Geo-coding enables integration of SAR data with GIS and other spatial datasets, allowing accurate overlay and analysis in real-world map projections. Read more
Radiometric Calibration
Corrects SAR data for system noise and geometry to represent true surface reflectivity. Radiometric calibration standardizes radar measurements across sensors, scenes, and acquisition times, making quantitative analysis and comparison possible. Read more
