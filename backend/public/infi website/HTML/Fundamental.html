Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) SAR uses a small antenna to transmit radar pulses, creating a synthetic aperture by combining multiple echoes. It provides high spatial resolution, independent of altitude.

SAR systems use an antenna of smaller physical dimensions, sending pulses from different positions as the platform advances. This technique simulates a synthetic aperture by integrating the echoes into a composite signal. The ability of SAR to work in all-weather conditions makes it invaluable for remote sensing applications.

Range, Azimuth, and Swath Range is perpendicular to the flight path, azimuth is parallel, and swath is the imaged area on Earth's surface.

The range direction is used to differentiate targets based on time delay, while the azimuth direction relies on Doppler frequency shifts. Swath width varies depending on the radar wavelength and platform altitude.
