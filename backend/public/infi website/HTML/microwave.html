Introduction to Microwave Remote Sensing

Microwave remote sensing is a quite effective technique that uses the electromagnetic waves within the range of microwave spectrum (wavelengths ranging from 1mm to 1m) to gather information about the Earth's surface. This kind of remote sensing is very exceptional because it can penetrate clouds, haze, and even vegetation, allowing it to provide accurate data regardless of weather conditions or time of day. These capabilities make microwave remote sensing valuable for applications where optical remote sensing techniques are less useful, such as for cloudy weather or nighttime observation.

Key Concepts of Microwave Remote Sensing

  • Active Microwave Remote Sensing: In active systems, the sensor generates and transmits its own microwave energy toward the Earth's surface and measures the energy reflected back.
    Sensor Used: Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)
  • Applications:
    • Analyzing urban growth patterns and monitor land use changes.
    • Identifying and tracking oil spills on the ocean surface using the smooth texture of oil against rough water.
  • Passive Microwave Remote Sensing: Passive systems do not emit any energy. Instead, they detect natural microwave radiation emitted by objects.
    Sensor Used: Radiometer
  • Applications:
    • Meteorology
    • Hydrology
    • Oceanography

Microwave Bands and Their Applications

Band Wavelength (in cm) Properties
P-Band 30-100 P-band can penetrate tree cover to reach the soil below.
X-Band 2.4 – 3.75 High frequency, short wavelength; suitable for detailed surface imaging.
C-Band 3.75 – 7.5 Moderate penetration capability; balances between detail and surface depth.
L-Band 15 – 30 Long wavelength; penetrates vegetation and soil, useful for subsurface studies.
S-Band 7.5 – 15 Medium wavelength; better penetration of atmosphere for weather observations.
Ku, Ka Band 1.1 – 2.4 Very short wavelength; high resolution for fine-scale surface detection.

Applications of Microwave Remote Sensing

  • Flood Mapping: It detects and monitors flooded areas, providing near-real-time information for emergency management.
  • Soil Moisture Monitoring: Measures soil moisture levels critical for agriculture and drought assessment.
  • Sea Ice Monitoring: Tracks the extent, thickness, and drift of sea ice for navigation as well as climate research.
  • Urban Deformation Monitoring: It detects land subsidence and deformation of infrastructure, such as building or bridge stability.
