What is Remote Sensing?

Remote sensing is the acquisition of information about an object or phenomenon without making physical contact with the object. This is done using satellite or aircraft-based sensor technologies to detect and classify objects on Earth.

Why Remote Sensing?

Remote sensing allows for the collection of data over large areas and in inaccessible regions, providing critical information for a variety of applications including environmental monitoring, disaster management, and resource exploration.

Types of Remote Sensing

Optical Remote Sensing: Uses visible, near-infrared, and short-wave infrared sensors to detect reflected sunlight.

Microwave Remote Sensing: Uses radar to penetrate clouds and collect data in all weather conditions.

Thermal Remote Sensing: Detects heat emitted from the Earth's surface.

General Applications

Remote sensing is used in a variety of fields including agriculture, forestry, land-use planning, environmental monitoring, and disaster management. It helps in mapping land cover, monitoring deforestation, assessing crop health, and managing natural disasters like floods and earthquakes.
